Comments from the Editors and Reviewers:

Reviewer: 1

Comments to the Author
This study is the first to systematically introduce the conditional diffusion probabilistic model into the InSAR phase unwrapping problem, achieving a methodological paradigm shift from "deterministic regression" to "conditional generation with progressive restoration," which represents a significant original contribution at the academic level. In terms of technical implementation, the authors design a Wavelet Window Frequency Decomposed Cross Attention (WWFCA) module, which achieves collaborative modeling of local details and global structures through frequency-domain decomposition and cross-frequency attention mechanisms; the network architecture is well-targeted and physically intuitive. Meanwhile, the Dual-Channel Concatenation strategy elegantly and effectively resolves the phase periodicity representation problem. The experimental section is reasonably comprehensive, covering both synthetic and real-world datasets, multi-noise-level comparisons, and ablation studies, with systematic comparisons against mainstream methods including DLPU, U3Net, Restormer, and Uformer; on the Synthetic dataset, MAE and NRMSE are improved by 55.7% and 42.5%, respectively—results that are impressive. The paper is fluently written and logically coherent. Nevertheless, several issues remain to be addressed.
(1) The paper repeatedly emphasizes the ability of diffusion models to characterize the inherent "one-to-many" uncertainty in phase unwrapping as a core advantage (see Pages 1, 2, and 4). However, the experimental section reports only point-estimation metrics such as MAE and RMSE, without any empirical analysis of uncertainty quantification. Several critical questions remain unanswered: Do multiple samplings of the same input yield diverse unwrapping results? What is the spatial distribution of prediction variance? Can prediction confidence serve as an indicator of unwrapping reliability? To address this gap, the authors are encouraged to provide empirical evidence of uncertainty quantification—for example, by performing multiple samplings on the same input and reporting the spatial distribution of standard deviation, or by analyzing the correlation between variance and error in low-coherence regions. Alternatively, if the current framework does not yet support such analysis, the authors should explicitly acknowledge that the uncertainty modeling potential has not been fully exploited and should be clearly identified as future work. Without such evidence, the paper's claims regarding the advantages of uncertainty modeling remain empirically unsupported.
(2) The multi-step sampling required during the inference stage of diffusion models (5 steps in this paper, though the model is trained with 1000 steps) results in computational efficiency that is substantially lower than that of single-step regression models such as DLPU and Restormer. However, the paper only qualitatively mentions "relatively high computational cost" in the conclusion (Page 38) without providing any quantitative comparison data—such as inference time per image, parameter counts, or FLOPs. It is recommended that a quantitative computational efficiency comparison table be included to clarify the computational cost entailed by the accuracy improvements. Even if the proposed method achieves significantly higher accuracy at the cost of one to two orders of magnitude in computational expense—a trade-off that may be acceptable—readers still need to know the specific figures to assess its practical feasibility. Furthermore, given that Section 3.4 states that "WWFCA is applied only to the low-resolution layers to control computational overhead," it would be advisable to quantify the actual acceleration effect achieved by this design (e.g., reduction in inference time or FLOPs).
(3) In Section 3.2, the authors formulate the forward and reverse processes of the diffusion model within the SDE framework (Eqs. 7–10) and introduce the conditioning information c (the wrapped phase). However, it remains unclear what the "data distribution" Pdata(x) specifically refers to in this context. Does it denote the distribution of the true continuous phase or that of the denoised phase? The normalization described on Page 19 maps the true phase to the range [−1,1], yet the mathematical framework does not explicitly clarify the relationship between x0 and the true phase φ. Clarification of this fundamental relationship is essential for a complete understanding of the theoretical formulation.
(4) The ablation study (Table 4) reveals an anomalous trend: when DCC is used alone, MAE deteriorates from 0.0370 to 0.0636 (an increase of 72%), while RMSE and PGE improve simultaneously. This counterintuitive pattern is not adequately explained in the main text; the authors merely note that "DCC alone may partially sacrifice pixel-level accuracy" (Pages 35–36), without delving into the underlying mechanisms. Why does the smooth cos/sin representation lead to MAE degradation? Potential explanations include: the dual-channel representation doubles the dimensionality of the conditional space, thereby increasing the difficulty of pixel-level precise regression; or DCC alters the feature distribution of the conditional input, thereby affecting the training dynamics. It is recommended that the authors provide a deeper analysis of this anomalous behavior, particularly by examining the discrepancy between the diffusion model's loss function (noise prediction error) and the final phase MAE, to elucidate the underlying mechanism.
(5) The visual results presented in Figure 12 are not sufficiently interpreted in the text. It is recommended that, under high-noise conditions (e.g., 0 dB and 5 dB), the visual comparison across methods should focus particularly on their performance in phase discontinuity regions and their ability to suppress noise-induced artifacts. Additionally, it would be worthwhile to examine whether the multi-step denoising process of the diffusion model yields a visually smoother phase field and, if so, whether this smoothing entails a risk of over-smoothing—that is, the loss of true high-frequency phase details. A more thorough visual analysis would substantially strengthen the qualitative evidence supporting the method's robustness.


Reviewer: 2

Comments to the Author
This paper presents an innovative framework for InSAR phase unwrapping by formulating the process as a conditional diffusion process guided by wrapped phase inputs. The integration of the Dual-Channel Trigonometric Code and the Wavelet-Window Frequency Cross-Attention module provides a solid technical foundation for processing complex phase fields. I have read carefully the text and hope that my review helps the authors to improve the manuscript. I recommend addressing the following specific concerns and recommendations:
1. In the Abstract and Conclusion sections, the authors emphasize significant performance gains (e.g., a 55.7% reduction in MAE and a 42.5% reduction in NRMSE), but these improvements are established solely on noise-free synthetic datasets. As shown in Table 2, when evaluated on real-world data (InSAR-DLPU), the proposed method yields a higher MAE (2.4095) and PGE (0.2226) compared to the baseline DLPU model (MAE 2.2887, PGE 0.1917). The authors should objectively reflect this performance degradation on real scenes in both the Abstract and Conclusion, and provide an in-depth discussion analyzing why the model exhibits limited generalizability on complex real interferograms.
2. The proposed diffusion model requires 5 sampling iterations during inference (Table 1), which inherently increases computational overhead and memory consumption compared to single-step feedforward networks like DLPU. As a supplement to the comparative experiments, it is recommended to add the computation volume (FLOPs), inference latency, and GPU memory usage for each model under identical spatial resolutions. This will allow readers to intuitively evaluate the trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency.
3. In Section 4.4, regarding the noise sensitivity evaluation (0" dB"∼30" dB" ), please explicitly clarify the noise model used in the experiments. Specifically, state whether the noise corresponds to additive Gaussian phase noise or multiplicative speckle noise in the complex interferometric domain, as this distinction is essential for InSAR phase processing.
4. To intuitively demonstrate the framework's capability in mitigating phase aliasing, it is suggested to include 1D cross-sectional profile comparison plots across regions featuring high phase gradients (e.g., steep topography or fault dislocations).

Reviewer: 3

Comments to the Author
This manuscript proposes a conditional diffusion framework combined with wavelet-frequency cross-attention for interferometric phase unwrapping. The proposed architecture is interesting, and the results on the synthetic dataset show encouraging improvements over several deep-learning baselines. However, I have several fundamental concerns regarding the physical validity of the phase unwrapping formulation and the validation of the method on real InSAR observations. These issues currently prevent the reported results from fully supporting the main claims of the manuscript.

1. The wrapped phase is used as conditional information, but I could not identify an explicit constraint ensuring that the predicted unwrapped phase satisfies 𝒲(𝜙_pred)= 𝜙_wrapped modulo 2π. Without such a constraint or verification, a plausible continuous phase field is not necessarily a valid unwrapping of the observation. Please quantitatively evaluate rewrapping or cycle consistency.
2. The manuscript describes the 30,000/1,000 InSAR-DLPU train/test pairs as real-world data. However, these pairs are simulated from SRTM-derived terrain, while a separate set of 100 real TanDEM-X interferogram/absolute-phase pairs is provided for generalization testing. This distinction should be clearly addressed.
3. The practical applicability of the proposed approach cannot be sufficiently established using only the simulated portions of the benchmark datasets. Quantitative evaluation on the real TanDEM-X subset of InSAR-DLPU, or another independent real InSAR dataset with a defensible reference, would be important.
4. Section 3.2 states adopts clean-sample prediction, whereas Section 3.5 refers to predicting the noise component and Section 4.3 describes a noise-prediction training paradigm. Please clarify the actual prediction target and make the mathematical formulation, implementation, and training description consistent.
5. A major motivation for adopting a diffusion model is its ability to represent the one-to-many ambiguity and uncertainty of phase unwrapping. However, the experiments report only deterministic accuracy measures. Multiple realizations for the same wrapped phase and a quantitative assessment of predictive uncertainty would be needed to demonstrate this claimed advantage.
6. Phase-unwrapping solutions can differ by a global integer multiple of 2 π. Please explain how the phase reference or global offset is handled before calculating MAE and RMSE. 
7. The experimental comparison is restricted to deep-learning approaches. Since the study proposes a phase-unwrapping method, comparison with established approaches such as MCF, tree method, or quality-guided unwrapping methods under the same conditions would provide an important practical benchmark.
8. The manuscript emphasizes robustness in low-coherence areas, while the wrapped phase appears to be the primary observational condition used by the model. Since the phase itself becomes unreliable in very low-coherence regions, it would be useful to evaluate performance as a function of coherence and to consider coherence information or reliability masks in the formulation.
9. On the InSAR-DLPU test set, the proposed method does not outperform DLPU in all metrics, particularly MAE and PGE, and its performance also deteriorates substantially at the lowest SNR. The statements regarding consistent superiority, strong generalization, and practical applicability therefore appear stronger than what is currently demonstrated and should be supported by additional real-data experiments or moderated accordingly.
